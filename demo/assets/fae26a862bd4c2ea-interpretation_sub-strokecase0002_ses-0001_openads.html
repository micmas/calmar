White-matter disconnection: normative lesion-based modelsOpen sectionClose section

This section shows white-matter tracts that may be interrupted beyond the visible lesion. Open a tract to inspect its anatomical context and any group-level findings linked to it.

The BCBToolkit map estimates possible disconnection from the lesion and reference connectivity. A tract label appears in this summary when ≥50% of its atlas voxels have a map value above 0.5. The calculation describes atlas voxel coverage under the model. For a region linked to several atlas labels or both sides, the report uses the highest measured label-side value. Relationship counts summarise the stored group findings for each tract: association means a group-level study reported a relationship; inverse association means the stored relationship runs in the opposite direction; null means the study did not find the association; mixed means results were inconsistent; and other / unclassified means no display direction was assigned. A cross-reference means the same symptom target also has direct-damage evidence from a different anatomy in this participant’s lesion-location section. It is not independent or additive evidence and does not predict the participant’s symptoms.

How to read the evidenceOpen sectionClose section

Display signal is CALMaR’s project-specific ordering heuristic, not a formal evidence grade or participant-level prediction. Its basis is explained beside the outcome summaries.

Relationship is what a study reported between the region or measure and the outcome: association, inverse association, no association or mixed results. Evidence strength is the extractor’s reading of the finding. Extraction confidence is confidence that the paper was transcribed and interpreted correctly. These are distinct.

Finding counts are descriptive. They do not measure certainty, and findings do not necessarily represent independent studies or independent cohorts. Group associations do not predict this participant’s outcome.
