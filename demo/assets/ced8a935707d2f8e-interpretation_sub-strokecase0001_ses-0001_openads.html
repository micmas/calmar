Lesion location: what the evidence saysOpen sectionClose section

This section connects the participant’s lesion location to published group-level findings about symptoms. Aphasia diagnoses, subtype labels and other non-symptom targets are excluded: this report does not infer the presence or type of aphasia from a lesion. Start with the symptom summaries, then open a damaged region to inspect each supporting, null or mixed finding.

A region counts as damaged when it holds ≥5% of the lesion or ≥25% of it is destroyed. Regions are matched to the knowledge base through the curated atlas mapping (stroke-aphasia-kb/atlas_mapping/). Of the knowledge base's 461 location findings, 57 concern regions this lesion damages before the symptom-only display filter; 43 symptom findings are shown. 346 concern regions it does not damage, and 10 concern regions no atlas can represent (e.g. the extreme capsule).

Lesion size first: this lesion is 5.9 mL and the matched findings concern 5 damaged knowledge-base regions. Larger lesions damage more regions and therefore match more findings, whatever their location, and lesion volume is itself one of the stronger correlates of severity. Read the number of findings below as partly a measure of lesion size.

What does the display signal mean?

Project-specific display heuristic, not an evidence grade. It is used only to order these summaries. The calculation combines the proportion of each matched region damaged with the stored finding strength, study evidence class, extraction confidence and relationship direction. Lesion-mapping findings whose study did not control (or did not state controlling) lesion volume, or used no stated multiple-comparison correction, count for less. Finding counts do not measure certainty, and findings may come from the same study or overlapping cohorts.

Display signal: a project-specific ordering heuristic, not an evidence grade. It combines lesion overlap with stored finding strength, study evidence class, extraction confidence and relationship direction. Counts do not measure certainty, and records may share studies or cohorts.

Primary display relevance criterion: a symptom appears in the main list when its matched findings span at least 2 independent evidence groups (citation keys, with papers from the same or overlapping cohorts counted once) and 2 distinct anatomy mappings after KB synonyms are merged. Association, inverse, null, mixed and unclassified findings all count toward this display threshold. This is a scanning rule, not a certainty threshold; cohort overlap is only as complete as the knowledge base records it.

White-matter disconnection: normative lesion-based modelsOpen sectionClose section

This section shows white-matter tracts that may be interrupted beyond the visible lesion. Open a tract to inspect its anatomical context and any group-level findings linked to it.

The BCBToolkit map gives, for every voxel, the probability that its fibres pass through the lesion. A tract counts as disconnected when ≥50% of its voxels have a probability above 0.5. This catches tracts cut by the lesion away from where it lies, which direct overlap misses. Relationship counts summarise the stored group findings for each tract: association means a group-level study reported a relationship; inverse association means the stored relationship runs in the opposite direction; null means the study did not find the association; mixed means results were inconsistent; and other / unclassified means no display direction was assigned. A cross-reference means the same symptom target also has direct-damage evidence from a different anatomy in this participant’s lesion-location section. It is not independent or additive evidence and does not predict the participant’s symptoms.

How to read the evidenceOpen sectionClose section

Display signal is CALMaR’s project-specific ordering heuristic, not a formal evidence grade or participant-level prediction. Its basis is explained beside the outcome summaries.

Relationship is what a study reported between the region or measure and the outcome: association, inverse association, no association or mixed results. Evidence strength is the extractor’s reading of the finding. Extraction confidence is confidence that the paper was transcribed and interpreted correctly. These are distinct.

Finding counts are descriptive. They do not measure certainty, and findings do not necessarily represent independent studies or independent cohorts. Group associations do not predict this participant’s outcome.

An “atlas match includes a documented judgement call” note links a knowledge-base region to atlas units using a curated decision recorded in stroke-aphasia-kb/atlas_mapping/README.md.

WAB/WAB-R profile conversion: where a recorded type informed the domain table, + relatively preserved kept severity unassigned and − impaired became a “mild profile floor”. Neither is a measured domain severity.
Stage badge: Acute <7 d · Subacute 7 d–6 mo · Chronic >6 mo. A therapy-stage warning identifies evidence from a different recovery stage; open it for detail. Rules and their sources: stroke-aphasia-kb/scripts/aphasia_rules.py.
